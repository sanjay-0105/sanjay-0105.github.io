Framework for Simulation-Based Assessment of First- and Last-Mile Fleet Sizing Strategies for a Multimodal Transit Interchange

M.Tech in Civil Engineering · Indian Institute of Science, Bengaluru
Supervisor: Dr. Tarun Rambha

An agent-based simulation in AnyLogic for sizing auto-rickshaw fleets at Yeshwantpur Railway Station, Bengaluru, where mode choice across walking, auto, bus, metro and private vehicles is modelled with a multinomial logit model calibrated on IRCTC reservation data and BMTC GTFS schedules. The results show that last-mile bus use is held back by service gaps at the interchange rather than by preference, and that waiting time is the strongest policy lever, which links fleet sizing and bus scheduling as decisions that should be planned together.

M.Tech

2021–2022

Dispersion Modelling of Gaseous Air Pollutants from the Digboi Oil Refinery

M.Tech in Environmental Engineering · Indian Institute of Technology Guwahati
Supervisor: Prof. Sharad Gokhale

This thesis estimates how much additional pollution the airshed around the Digboi oil refinery, Assam, can safely absorb. Seasonal mixing height and ventilation coefficient were derived from meteorological data, and AERMOD was used to model SO₂ and NOₓ dispersion from the refinery's stacks at existing and maximum permissible loads. Although ventilation is low throughout the year and monsoon is the most critical season, predicted concentrations remain well below NAAQS limits, with the highest values 2.5–6.5 km downwind in the predominantly north-east wind direction, near the railway station area.

Nov–Dec 2025 · M.Tech course project · Dr. Tarun Rambha, CiSTUP, IISc

An Exact Algorithm for the Multiple Vehicle Pickup and Delivery Problem

Implemented Lu and Dessouky's exact integer-programming formulation for the pickup and delivery problem, with valid inequalities and a branch-and-cut algorithm using a prediction heuristic. Tested on Solomon's random and clustered datasets, it finds optimal routes for up to 15 customers within an hour. Clustered instances with pickups and deliveries in different clusters proved the hardest.

Oct–Nov 2025 · M.Tech course project (CP219), team of 3 · Dr. Puneet Rathore, CPS, IISc

Unsupervised Learning for Cyber Intrusion Detection in Power Substations

Built an unsupervised system that flags cyber-attacks on power-substation network messages without needing labelled attack data, by engineering timing and sequence features and comparing seven anomaly-detection models. The best model (One-Class SVM) caught 88% of attacks with under 2% false alarms, fast enough for real-time use.

Sept 2025 · M.Tech course project, team of 2 · Prof. Vijay Kovvali, CiSTUP, IISc

Last-Mile Connectivity Analysis and Visualisation for Metro Systems

A data-driven study of how metro riders in Bengaluru, Delhi and Nagpur complete the last mile, combining interactive Power BI dashboards with QGIS maps of every station. It shows walking dominates everywhere, longer last-mile trips also cost more, and pinpoints the stations that most need better pedestrian access and feeder services.

Jan–Apr 2025 · M.Tech course project (Choice Modelling), team of 3 · Prof. Abdul Rawoof Pinjari, IISc

Mode Choice Modelling for the Swiss Metro

A choice-modelling study of how Swiss travellers would choose between car, train and a proposed high-speed Swiss Metro, comparing logit, nested logit and mixed logit models on stated-preference survey data. Policy tests show cheaper or more frequent trains can shift demand away from the metro, and travellers value time savings at about 0.83 Swiss francs per minute.

  • Discrete Choice Modelling
  • Mixed Logit
  • Stated Preference
  • Transport Policy
